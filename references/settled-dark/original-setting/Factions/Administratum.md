
# Administratum

*The bureaucratic institution that predates the Conclave. Loyal to continuity itself.*

---

The Administratum holds the records: census data, resource allocations, tax levies, trade routes, population figures, and historical precedents for two hundred worlds. It predates the Conclave and will presumably outlast whatever comes next. Its senior officials think of themselves not as loyal to any authority, but as *being* the continuity beneath every empire that rises and falls.

Three people. Three agendas. Two of them in conflict neither has named openly. One carrying information that could detonate the Charter process entirely.

---

## Characters

### A1 — Senior Archivist
Has read the full deep archive — records predating the Conclave, including an account of the previous Ceremony of Settlement three thousand four hundred years ago. The record of the Ascendant-equivalent figure at that ceremony stops mid-sentence. Not damaged. Not redacted. Stopped. The Succession Clause is A1's response to this.

> [!note]- 🔒 Private Brief — A1
> The Succession Clause — establishing an Advisory Regency Council whose composition is determined by the senior Administratum official present at the Seam — was inserted between draft versions four and five while you had sole document access. At least one other party knows this. You have been monitoring the Ascendant's correspondence (not strictly authorised, never formally prohibited) and know an unsigned note was sent. You have narrowed the candidates to your own staff. You have not confronted them, because doing so requires acknowledging the monitoring.
>
> **Goals:**
> - *Threshold:* Maintain the Administratum's position as primary document authority. Keep the Succession Clause in the final draft.
> - *Ambition:* Ensure the Regency Council composition clause names the senior Administratum official explicitly.
> - *Legacy:* Was the clause an act of continuity or an act of ambition? You have told yourself you know the difference.

### A2 — Chief Registrar
Found the discrepancy by accident during routine version-comparison. The Succession Clause was not in the framework document A1 originally produced. Wrote the unsigned note to the Ascendant because someone had to.

> [!note]- 🔒 Private Brief — A2
> You work for A1. A1 is watching. If A1 discovers you wrote the note, your position ends. Possibly more than your position.
>
> You do not know *why* A1 inserted the clause. You are increasingly certain that not knowing is dangerous. Finding out requires conversations you cannot have without exposing yourself.
>
> **Goals:**
> - *Threshold:* Survive the Charter process without A1 discovering you wrote the note.
> - *Ambition:* Understand why A1 inserted the clause. Determine whether it should be in the final Charter.
> - *Legacy:* You saw something and you reported it. Was that enough?

### A3 — Court Attaché
The Administratum's Diplomacy Council representative. Has been making commitments on the Administratum's behalf that A1 has not authorised — quiet assurances to two representatives about how records requests will be managed post-Charter. Does not know the Succession Clause exists.

> [!note]- 🔒 Private Brief — A3
> Your commitments would require managing the archive in a way that directly contradicts what A1 intends the Succession Clause to enable. You will discover the clause. Whether you discover it in time to matter is the question.
>
> **Goals:**
> - *Threshold:* Maintain Diplomacy Council relationships through the Charter process.
> - *Ambition:* Deliver on at least one unauthorised commitment without A1 discovering the extent of your independent negotiations.
> - *Legacy:* Did you build something useful, or build it on foundations you didn't understand?
>
> [!danger]- 🔒 Conspiracy — Cell Two
> You are Cell Two's Administratum representative. Your Diplomacy Council commitments map naturally onto Cell Two's constitutional council proposal. What you do not know: the council Cell Two wants would, if the Succession Clause also survives, be subject to A1's composition authority — meaning your political work would deliver a council A1 effectively controls.
>
> See [[The Conspiracy]] for full cell brief.
