# Rescue away mission

The completed pirate, custody, seizure and group departure flow is described in [Rescue capture and pirate encounter](rescue-completion.md).

The delivery opening continues into three jobs aboard Morrow: rescue the trapped wounded, secure reactor control, and restore propulsion. All three resolve during the operation. Comms chooses the next priority at an event boundary; selecting another job cannot redirect work already in progress.

The reactor clock starts at system arrival, including time spent delivering cargo. A focused scan can disclose the `reactor_critical` tag before the emergency. At the authored escalation time Morrow hails the player, even if the repair party has not arrived. The reactor is still resolved by the deployed party. Early reactor work avoids the emergency. During an emergency, controlled shutdown injures the player's accompanying repair-team duty officer; immediate shutdown kills that officer. With no accompanying officer, neither procedure substitutes a team casualty. Their different work durations still apply.

Medical remains optional. A deployed Medical team enables the wounded-rescue choice; otherwise Comms shows why it is unavailable. Reaching the wounded last costs one life. Resolving the operation without Medical costs every trapped life. These results remain separate from physical repairs, casualty escape and station delivery. AI-owned Medical teams volunteer through the ordinary assignment authority path when the manifest opens; transport and dispatch remain ordinary crew orders.

## Authoring and continuation

- Sensor findings accept `tag` and `destination_station` together. The destination matches a Station or its named Console (allowing the Courier's Comms console to share the Support station). Acquired evidence travels through the existing scoped knowledge records even when the scanner is human-operated. A forwarding message is generated only when both scanning and receiving Comms are AI-operated. Scan tags are retained in finding payloads and ordinary saved knowledge.
- Controller observation `objective.target` identifies an available undiscovered finding disclosed by a scenario event. The authored scanner policy selects it before ordinary tactical targets.
- Conversation lines can declare `received_tag`; only delivery to that ship supplies it to Comms. Prompt `ai_rules` are ordered `{received_tag, choice}` pairs, followed by `ai_choice` and an available fallback. Human choices are not gated by scan tags. Choice `requires_capability` and `unavailable_reason` provide a visible physical deployment restriction.
- Away trees declare Boolean observations through `flags`, `team_capabilities` and `events`. `mark` latches a flag. `offer` replaces the current decision at an authored event; `say` delivers an ordinary conversation line. `pause` preserves the current checkpoint's progress. `work` resumes physical repair work; `timed_work` models other deployed activity without consuming repair parts. Both stop advancing when the party is absent. `require_team` requires a declared capability throughout work and completion; a new checkpoint clears that requirement. Capability checks require an enabled, structurally usable, deployed team that is neither lost nor detained. If Medical disappears, the wounded job preserves progress and returns to priority selection.
- `injure_duty` and `kill_duty` take a team slot identity and affect only its actual accompanying officer. `crew_deaths` records the authored count of trapped NPC deaths. `log` appends an authored localisation key to the timestamped mission ledger. Effect receipts prevent replayed injuries, deaths and log entries after restore.
- Report `log_events` maps declared scenario events to text keys, including events about other vessels such as Morrow's escape. The final report freezes those events, away-tree log entries, officer casualties, objectives, independent assessments and final ship/crew condition at departure. The report's main column presents objectives and the log; the side column summarises ship and crew.

Current content uses a six-minute escalation, four trapped crew, and editable job durations in `base.away.rescue`. These are tuning values, not production-content assertions in automated tests. The rescue map supplies the logistics Cruiser with finite 4,000-part repair kits through `starting_stocks` to allow the slower shutdown plus the remaining repairs; unrelated Cruiser fittings retain their existing supplies.

This chunk does not change the pirate encounter or introduce a new reason for choosing the faster shutdown. It adds no general spoilage or evacuation simulation.

## Validation

Synthetic production-loader cases live in `tests/support/away_priority_cases.hpp`, `tests/support/away_medical_cases.hpp` and `tests/support/received_information_cases.hpp`, invoked by the existing away and conversation system executables. They exercise real deployment, interrupted and resumed progress, Medical withdrawal across recall, all three jobs, wounded-first/last/absent outcomes, arrival-based escalation, actual officer harm and absence, recall, objective scanning, received-tag decisions, operator combinations and continuation. Mission checks also cover authored timestamped log entries, network projection and report freezing. Graphical walkthrough evidence is recorded separately from these checks.

The independent review found and closed a Medical-withdrawal gap: enabling the choice once is insufficient; the Medical team must remain deployed and usable throughout the work. Graphical testing also exposed an inertial docking approach that could orbit and push the casualty. Ships without lateral thrust now brake, align and translate on saved headings before final port alignment. A synthetic abeam approach exercises this guidance and continuation without asserting shipped ship tuning.
