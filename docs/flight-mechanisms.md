# Flight mechanisms

`phoenix.flight` in `src/flight/flight.cppm` and `flight_definitions.cpp` supplies the shared typed flight definition and production compiler, without simulation or graphics dependencies. `src/simulation/flight.hpp` and `flight.cpp` supply per-fitted-System runtime execution inside `phoenix.simulation`. They use the existing integer direction/vector math and body state. The adapter neither integrates position nor resolves collision, and cannot fetch world or contact truth.

## Authoring

A flight spec selects `kind`: `engine`, `steering`, `lateral`, `boost` or `impulse`. Numeric distances/speeds/acceleration use mm, mm/s, mm/s²; headings/rates use millidegrees and millidegrees/s. Resource rates are authored units per second. The simulation rate is 20 Hz.

Common fields are `max_speed_mm_s`, `reverse_speed_mm_s`, `acceleration_mm_s2`, `deceleration_mm_s2`, `turn_mdeg_s`, `lateral_speed_mm_s`, `lateral_acceleration_mm_s2`, `assist_lateral`, `power_rate`, `heat_rate` and `match_stale_ticks`. Reverse defaults to zero; passive damping corrects unrequested drift. Engines and legacy untargeted boost/impulse actuators require positive forward speed and acceleration; steering requires positive turn rate; lateral requires positive lateral speed and acceleration. Each fitted actuator has its own state, demand and grant.

An engine may additionally define `boost:{acceleration_multiplier_permille,speed_multiplier_permille,power_rate,heat_rate}` and `impulse:{charge_ticks,acceleration_multiplier_permille,speed_multiplier_permille,steering_permille,charge_power_rate,power_rate,heat_rate}`. A legacy untargeted standalone boost/impulse uses the same mode fields at its root, with its own speed and acceleration. Compiler limits apply after multipliers, so authored values cannot exceed supported integer arithmetic. Combined power/heat rates remain finite.

## Commands and resources

### Targeted fitted modifiers

The shipped boost/impulse fittings now use `targets:[{system,property,multiplier_permille}]`. Supported properties are `max_speed_mm_s`, `acceleration_mm_s2` and `turn_mdeg_s`. Resolve references to typed equipment ordinals during loading, including assembly-local renaming. Missing/incompatible targets, modifier-to-modifier bindings and multiplied values outside fixed-point limits fail verification. Each property uses the strongest paid bonus. A targeted modifier has its own `power_rate`/`heat_rate`, produces no independent thrust and cannot replace target operating power. Held boost raises available limits; throttle/manual demand still controls engine actuation.

For targeted impulse author `charge_ticks`, `charge_energy`, `minimum_reserve`, `steering_permille`, `power_rate` and `heat_rate`. Minimum reserve defaults to charge energy, must cover it and must fit the connected `power_store`. Admission checks actual energy and store/equipment availability. Each charge tick pays the difference between cumulative rounded-up energy entitlement and expenditure already recorded; an unfunded slice pauses work. Overdrive cannot accelerate these charge ticks or reduce total energy. Cancellation discards progress without refund. Completion enables continuous bound propulsion on the next tick. Boost is suppressed throughout charging/transit; steering is restricted only during transit. Collision, disablement and insufficient operating grants end transit without clamping velocity.

Impulse start/cancel uses a Boolean request channel (`semantics:request`): true starts and false cancels. Each explicit request has a fresh identity. Retransmission cannot restart completed/cancelled work, while a new start can charge again after a collision without first toggling an obsolete setting off. Shipped layouts and legacy-to-Controller conversion author this contract explicitly.

`lateral_direction` accepts `port`, `starboard` or `both` (default). Unsupported directional effort produces no powered thrust. `assist_lateral` remains readable for compatibility; passive damping replaces its counter-drift work. Legacy self-contained engine modes and untargeted actuators remain readable for older authored packs; shipped propulsion assemblies use explicit targets.

Modifier allocation previews a copy of the resource network, then advances the authoritative network exactly once with identical requests. An active requested modifier reserves full operating duty on enabled targets, so changes to guidance effort cannot obtain unpaid bonuses. The paid fraction scales each boost's increment above 1000; target actuation separately uses its own grant. Charging uses ordinary atomic discrete requests in stable System order when a battery is shared. Previewing cannot debit authoritative energy, coolant, heat or random progression.

Controller observations `navigation.lateral_thrust` and `navigation.lateral_safe` propose signed avoidance/counter-thrust and report feasibility using frozen permitted contacts and reported lateral capability. Only AI-owned lateral channels contribute. The tree writes its own command section; shipped trees publish avoidance intent through normal messaging. Existing navigation fallback remains available when neither lateral corridor is feasible. This works while other Controllers on the same Console remain human-operated.

Q/E and gamepad shoulders hold lateral thrust; Shift and the west gamepad button hold boost. I and gamepad Start start/cancel impulse. Touch/pointer release is momentary. A semantic invoke toggles a held control to provide equivalent press/release actions for assistive technology. Both lateral buttons held means neutral; releasing one resumes the other. Focus/device loss and operator-generation changes clear held controls. Readouts distinguish requested/effective boost, charging progress/expenditure, readiness and cancellation reasons.

`apply_flight` validates a typed command against the fitted capability before changing a copied state. Speed supports the authored reverse limit. Direct longitudinal and steering axes require manual mode; lateral input requires a fitted lateral actuator. Assistance initialises speed from the current forward velocity and heading from the current attitude, and clears held controls. Throttle/manual input cancels Match Speed; steering does not.

`observe_flight` optionally accepts an already permitted contact observation. Call it before `flight_demand` to quote the correct current target. The demand reports actual required effort and power/heat rates. The resource owner grants a fraction in 0..1000; `step_flight` scales actual acceleration, turn, heat and charge progress by that fraction. A zero grant cannot create thrust or charge. The adapter does not allocate energy or coolant itself.

Both assisted and manual flight use a desired velocity based on actual hull heading, signed requested speed and deliberate fitted lateral intent. Manual axis fractions request proportional speed, and release requests zero. Each tick first projects current velocity onto the segment from zero to desired velocity, then moves toward that closest point by at most `deceleration_mm_s2 / 20`. Damping is passive: it is independent of propulsion enablement, damage, power and heat and cannot add speed. Zero desired velocity damps to rest. Powered acceleration then adds velocity along the desired direction, retaining the quadratic launch curve and clipping the forward component at the desired speed. Steering changes attitude without instantly rotating velocity. Residual transverse drift may temporarily raise total speed above the requested forward speed.

`deceleration_mm_s2` defaults to nominal acceleration for existing content. It is authored independently, is not scaled by boost/impulse or propulsion damage, and retains a saved fractional-tick remainder. Each fitted engine contributes in the existing stable actuator order. Deliberate lateral commands contribute to the desired vector; the old AI counter-drift and `assist_lateral` braking do not add duplicate correction. Lateral collision avoidance remains available. Disabled/unpowered propulsion retains the requested velocity segment for damping but cannot accelerate toward it.

Legacy untargeted boost adds sustained forward thrust within its authored speed limit. Impulse progresses from idle through paid charging to active forward transit. Charge completion enables active thrust on the following tick, avoiding active propulsion charged against a charging-only grant. Cancelling impulse clears charge progress and leaves velocity intact; passive damping subsequently changes that velocity within the authored deceleration limit. Boost and impulse are mutually exclusive within one actuator.

For separately fitted boost, impulse and steering Systems, the ship adapter enforces boost suppression and the active-transit steering limit before quoting/executing steering. These are explicit cross-System constraints, not hidden world lookups in the actuator.

## Match Speed knowledge

`FlightContactObservation` contains only contact identity, observed speed, observation tick and uncertainty. The adapter updates a matched target only from a newer observation of the same selected/matched contact, never from future observations or hidden velocity. It retains the last observed speed after the stale threshold and exposes staleness. Changing the selected contact cancels matching rather than silently following another actor. Restoring sight of the same contact refreshes an active match.

## Handover and continuity

`release_flight_inputs` clears held longitudinal/lateral/steering axes and boost without resetting velocity, current settings, observed knowledge or impulse work. Mode changes and command application never integrate position. Root authority routing invokes this on device/route loss and handover.

`save_flight` records all continuation fields, including signed integration remainders, observation metadata, charge progress and steering limit. `load_flight` checks the authored-definition signature, schema, exact types/ranges and consistent modes before returning a complete replacement state. A failed load leaves the caller's existing state untouched. Root world restore additionally validates saved observation ticks against the world clock and recovers live claims separately.

Flight schema **5** additionally records charge expenditure and cancellation reasons, verifies expenditure against progress, and rejects older versions with an explicit start-new-session error. Modifier effects are derived again from saved requests and the next paid allocation. Recovery clears held human commands through the existing authority transition while retaining impulse work.

Synthetic integration should exercise resource-limited acceleration/charging, signed reverse/lateral thrust, steering without velocity rotation, boost/impulse release with bounded passive braking, stale Match Speed and resumed continuation. Swept collision handling and production world/Console admission remain owned by their existing runtime paths; they are separate from this actuator adapter.

Nonzero fractional effort conservatively rounds its authored energy and heat rates upward to the next rate unit; payment scaling uses the same rule. This prevents prolonged tiny inputs from producing motion while every debit truncates to zero. The resource owner still retains its per-tick rate remainders.

## Swept collision integration

The existing collision unit also exposes `sweep_collide(a,b,start_a,start_b)`. The world builds broadphase bounds spanning each body's start and predicted end, preserving stable pair order. The sweep evaluates the actual circle/rotated-box separation over relative translation with the current tick's fixed orientation, finds first penetration at deterministic billionth-tick precision and reuses the existing collision response. It stops the movement segment at contact; tangential velocity continues on subsequent ticks. Position integration and objective crossings consume the resulting executed segment.

The minimum search uses convex squared circle distance, circle-to-box closest distance or maximum separating-axis gap. Bounded fraction refinement replaces travel-distance substeps, so a thin obstacle is not skipped by an arbitrary substep cap. No scene/render shape substitutes for the authored collider. `tests/system/swept_collision.cpp` exercises all supported shape pairs, rotated thin walls, opposing dynamic actors, a true broadphase near miss and recovery through real production-loader Worlds. Native/WASM execution and graphical journeys are recorded by the root verification stage.

## Player tuning and verification — 25 October 2026

Courier and Cruiser family forward/reverse limits are increased by 50%, nominal acceleration is doubled, and explicit passive deceleration is three times the former nominal braking rate. The current Courier uses 33,000 mm/s forward, 6,000 mm/s reverse, 7,000 mm/s² acceleration and 10,500 mm/s² damping. Each Cruiser drive uses 33,000 mm/s forward, 9,000 mm/s reverse, 3,200 mm/s² acceleration and 4,800 mm/s² damping. Turning rates and boost/impulse multipliers are unchanged; shared generic equipment templates are not retuned.

The legacy `motion_model: inertial` identifier continues to select resource-backed machinery, rather than indefinite uncorrected drift. The basic vector implementation uses the same segment damping rule. Navigation stopping-distance estimates use deceleration. Pursuit no longer adds its old counter-drift steering term; docking completes a latched final turn before replanning, and carried craft choose an interior recovery point within the actual 3D bay envelope.

Synthetic flight cases cover segment interior/endpoints, zero desired velocity, disabled propulsion without energy/heat use, manual release, steady-throttle turns, deliberate lateral intent with an unfunded lateral actuator, boost overspeed, multiple actuators and exact recovery including older saves without damping remainders. Native flight/docking/craft and their WASM equivalents pass. Native and WASM synthetic continuation traces match within the same run. The bounded native suite passes all 42 cases; four storage/process cases required an unsandboxed rerun. Logs are `build/velocity-flight-native-ctest.log`, `build/velocity-flight-native-rerun.log` and the `build/velocity-flight-*.trace` files. This is behavioral verification, not shipped-ship performance or physical-device acceptance.
